01

AI creation platform · BuzzFeed

BF Island

Leading the web engineering

BF Island was my favorite project at BuzzFeed. It was a nearly two-year effort involving several teams, and I led the web engineering: building reusable components, builders, and renderers, working with the iOS team, and discussing feature scope with design and product.

From Generator Generator to Island

The project grew out of Generator Generator, which my team and I had built earlier. My work connected JSON configuration and AI output to editor state, live previews, and canvas rendering. Shared components and configuration let us support different builders without copying an entire implementation for each one.

A product decision

For one meme format, product wanted the image model to generate text inside the image. The results weren’t reliable enough at the time, so I proposed letting people write their own message and checking it for profanity. The team agreed, and I implemented that approach.

04

Interactive advertising · Complex Networks

Nearly a decade of custom campaigns

From display ads to custom interactions

Over nearly ten years at Complex, I built an estimated 250+ branded campaigns. Some ran for a day; others ran for months. The work ranged from takeovers and run-of-site ads to animated banners, skins, overlays, expandable units, and audio experiences.

A few examples

For Samsung’s “X-ray” ad, moving a phone image revealed a hidden object. Horror campaigns added effects over article images. Hot Ones ads played audio clips from the show, and a Mountain Dew Baja Blast banner sent a motorbike jumping across the screen.

The “Superhero” format paired one 640×360 image with one 640×360 video, side by side on desktop and stacked on mobile. I also built toolbar ads with a 300×50 trigger that could expand to full width.

Making repeated work easier

When I joined Complex, we were rebuilding many of the same ad components for each campaign. I collected those components into templates and made them available to other engineers through GitHub.
